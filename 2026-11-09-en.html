Top Story · U.S. Midterm Elections

Democrats Sweep House and Senate, Trump Faces Opposition Congress for Final Two Years

Counting in the Nov. 3 midterms is nearly complete: Democrats have locked up 252 House seats and at least 54 in the Senate, in one of the Republicans' worst defeats in decades.

Source Reuters·AP count (compiled by Political.org)

In Associated Press-confirmed results as of Nov. 8, Democrats won 252 House seats to Republicans' 161, with 22 still unconfirmed — well past the 218-seat majority. In the Senate, Democrats won 20 of the 33 confirmed seats, flipping seven Republican seats in North Carolina, South Carolina, Texas, Ohio, Iowa, Kansas and Maine, with Alaska and Mississippi still unconfirmed. Democrats also won 22 of the 34 confirmed governor's races, flipping five Republican governorships.

Reuters interviews showed voter anger over high oil prices, the Iran war and Washington corruption was the key driver, and Trump himself conceded the Iran war had cost Republicans heavily. Rather than changing course after the defeat, he promoted hardline immigration architect Stephen Miller to White House chief of staff, while the MAGA movement began openly debating whether it has run its course. The new Congress takes office on Jan. 3, 2027.
