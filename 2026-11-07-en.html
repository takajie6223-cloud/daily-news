Top Story · U.S. Midterm Elections

Democrats Sweep House and Senate, Trump Faces Divided Government

In the Nov. 3 midterms, Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress — gaining at least 40 seats in the House, flipping at least seven Senate seats, and taking five governorships including Texas. High living costs and the Iran war were the main sources of voter anger.

Source Reuters·Le Monde data visualization·New Congress takes office in January

It was a sweeping “blue wave.” The seven flipped Senate states were Texas, Kansas, Maine, North Carolina, Ohio, South Carolina and Iowa — several long considered Republican strongholds, a sign that discontent had spread well beyond the Democratic base. A net House gain of at least 40 seats also gives Democrats real veto power over legislation and spending.

Washington moved quickly to reorganize after the vote: personnel shake-ups, policy shifts and a market repricing all at once. Democrats have vowed to push for restoring aid to Ukraine in the new Congress, while Trump's veto remains the biggest variable; meanwhile, expectations of tighter AI regulation triggered a sharp global stock selloff (see Economy & Markets). The divided-government fight has only just begun.

No. 08 · Global Markets

Global Stocks Hit by “AI Selloff”: Nasdaq in Correction, Nikkei in Bear Market, Korean Stocks Halted

Fears of tighter AI regulation after the Democratic sweep gave U.S. stocks their worst day since April 2025 on Thursday: the Nasdaq fell 5.5% to 24,315, entering correction territory, the S&P fell 4.4%, the Dow dropped 1,833 points, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index plunged over 12%, and about $3.2 trillion in U.S. market value was wiped out in a day.

The selloff spread to Asia-Pacific on Friday: the Nikkei fell 6% to 57,434, down more than 20% from its June high and into a bear market, South Korea's KOSPI fell more than 8% intraday and triggered a circuit breaker, and Taiwan stocks fell 4.5%. U.S. stocks rebounded slightly on Friday after strong jobs data, but the S&P still fell 2.7% for the week, its fourth straight weekly loss.

Source: Reuters · The Wall Street Journal · Reuters original · The Wall Street Journal

No. 09 · Jobs & Central Banks

U.S. Adds 165,000 Jobs in October, Beating Forecasts; Jobless Rate Falls to 4.1%; Bank of England Raises Rates to 4%

U.S. nonfarm payrolls rose by 165,000 in October, well above expectations of about 60,000, while the unemployment rate fell from 4.2% to 4.1% and August–September figures were revised up by a combined 84,000. Markets still expect a Fed rate hike in December, with odds around 65%–70%, and Tuesday's October CPI will be the key test. The Bank of England raised rates by 25 basis points to 4% on Thursday, its first hike since August 2023.

Oil Swings Wildly as Gold Posts Biggest Weekly Gain Since August

Brent crude briefly fell below $100 on Wednesday to about $94, its biggest weekly drop since 2020, as markets bet the midterm results would push toward de-escalation of the Iran war; it rebounded nearly 4% to $98.29 on Thursday after a Gulf tanker attack. Gold, lifted by safe-haven demand, rose 4.6% on the week to $4,064.50 an ounce, its biggest weekly gain since August.

As reported by Bloomberg and confirmed by Reuters on Nov. 6, OpenAI has closed the first $11 billion of a new funding round at a valuation of $1.25 trillion. Reuters reported on Nov. 6, citing The Information, that TSMC is considering entering advanced memory chips and has begun planning production of an advanced memory chip called 3D DRAM at the request of its biggest customer, Nvidia. The move comes as an AI-driven shortage of memory chips pushes prices up and designers scramble for new supply; if it proceeds, TSMC would compete with Samsung Electronics, SK hynix and Micron, though the production timeline is unclear.
