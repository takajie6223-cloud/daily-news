Front Page · Nvidia

Three Days After Resigning, Jensen Huang Is Reported to Be Discussing a Return as Nvidia CEO

The Financial Times reported on 15 November that Jensen Huang has discussed a possible return to Nvidia with confidants, as allies rally behind a comeback; the board has made no decision, and interim CEO Colette Kress remains in charge.

Source Financial Times (via News Made Clear and Mac Observer)

Huang resigned as Nvidia's president, CEO and board member on 12 November, ending 33 years at the helm. The board immediately named Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress as interim CEO and Harvey Jones as its first chair. The company gave no reason for the departure, and the board's independent investigation, prompted by reports about Huang's personal conduct, is still under way.

The comeback talk comes at a sensitive moment: Nvidia reports fiscal 2027 third-quarter results on 17 November, with revenue expected at about $108 billion, the new leadership's first major test. Nvidia shares closed on 13 November at $162.85, about 6% below their level before the resignation was announced.

No. 06 · China Prices

China's October PPI Rises 5.7%, a Four-Year High, While Consumer Inflation Misses Forecasts

China's producer price index rose 5.7% year on year in October, up from 5.0% in September and above the 5.3% median forecast in a Wall Street Journal poll — the eighth straight monthly rise, driven by energy prices and strong technology exports. Consumer prices rose only 0.7%, below the expected 0.8%, as consumption stayed weak.

No. 07 · Japan Economy

Japan's Q3 GDP Due This Morning: Growth Seen at Just 0.1%, With Consumption Set for First Fall in 10 Quarters

Japan's Cabinet Office releases preliminary Q3 GDP at 8:50 a.m. JST on Monday. A Reuters poll expects growth of just 0.1% quarter on quarter, or 0.3% annualised, sharply slower than Q2's 1.4%. Private consumption, over half of GDP, is forecast to fall 0.2% — its first decline in 10 quarters — with rebounding capital spending providing the main support.

With US Inflation Running Hot, a December Fed Hike Is Now 'Fully Priced In'
