Front Page · New U.S. Tariffs

Trump Proposes New Tariffs on 14 Trading Partners, With China Facing the Highest Rate at 20%

Citing "excess capacity," Washington proposes new tariffs on 14 economies including China, the EU and Japan, with top rates reaching 40%; a hearing is set for next January.

Source Reuters

The Office of the U.S. Trade Representative proposed on November 25, after an eight-month investigation, that 14 major trading partners had engaged in unfair practices involving "structural excess capacity," and moved to impose new tariffs. Chinese goods would face the highest rate, at 20%; if the U.S.-China trade truce is not extended, that rate could rise to 40%. Vietnam would face 20% or 35% depending on negotiations; India 25%; and Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, the EU and Switzerland 15%. The new tariffs would not take effect immediately, with a public hearing scheduled for January 25, 2027. Reuters analysis said the move aims to reshape global trade and loosen China's grip as the "factory of the world," while also adding leverage ahead of a December summit between Trump and Xi Jinping in Miami. China's foreign ministry rejected the U.S. accusations of unfair industrial practices but stopped short of threatening retaliation.
