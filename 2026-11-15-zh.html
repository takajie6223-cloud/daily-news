Front Page · U.S. Midterms

After a Midterm Landslide, Democrats Split Over Whether to Work With or Fight Trump

Gathering in Denver to celebrate their November 3 rout, Democrats split openly over a cost-of-living agenda and the road to 2028: moderates favour pragmatic lawmaking, while progressives demand bolder change.

Source Reuters

Democrats held their first major gathering since their midterm victory in Denver this week, Reuters reported on November 14. United in wanting relief from high living costs, the party split over how to deliver it, what lessons to draw from the win, and how combative to be toward President Donald Trump. Democrats are projected to hold at least 255 seats to the Republicans' 169 in the House, and at least 55 to 44 in the Senate. But Trump retains veto power for two more years, enough to block much of the Democratic agenda, while prospective 2028 candidates used the gathering to argue over how to turn electoral success into a governing programme.

U.S. Aviation

All U.S. Flights Grounded at Once as Air-Traffic Control Fault Strands Thousands

The Federal Aviation Administration issued a nationwide ground stop on the afternoon of November 13 after a communications failure in air-traffic control, lifting it more than four hours later. It was the longest nationwide stoppage since September 11 and only the third in U.S. aviation history, with more than 10,000 flights delayed and over 3,000 cancelled that day. Officials blamed overloaded legacy systems, found no sign of a cyberattack, and Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy urged Congress to fund a $50 billion air-traffic upgrade.

U.S. Wholesale Inflation Hits 6.5%, a Near Four-Year High, as a December Hike Looks All but Certain

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on November 13 that the Producer Price Index rose 0.3% in October from September and 6.5% from a year earlier — the fastest annual pace since November 2022 — while core PPI rose 5% year on year.
