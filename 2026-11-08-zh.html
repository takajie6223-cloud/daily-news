Top Story · U.S. Midterm Elections

Democrats Sweep House and Senate in Heavy Blow to Trump's Second Term

Democrats took both the Senate and the House in the Nov. 3 midterms, as voter anger over high prices, the Iran war and corruption powered a rare landslide.

Source Reuters

As counting stood on Friday night, Democrats led Republicans 54 to 44 in the Senate and 253 to 162 in the House, both past the majority threshold, with a handful of seats still uncalled. Democrats flipped at least seven Senate seats, including Republican strongholds such as Kansas, Texas and Iowa, and swept redrawn districts such as North Carolina.

Reuters analysis found Democrats had spotted voters shifting toward them on the economy early in the year, a trend accelerated by Trump's tariffs and higher oil prices from the Iran war. The White House then reshuffled its top team, with Trump naming Stephen Miller as chief of staff; Vice President Vance publicly blamed the Iran war for the defeat and called for ending the conflict quickly and focusing on affordability, positioning himself for 2028.

OpenAI Releases GPT-6 Cyber, Which Built Exploits for 948 Linux Kernel Flaws

OpenAI released its cybersecurity model GPT-6 Cyber on Nov. 5, saying it reproduced 1,724 Linux kernel vulnerabilities, developed working privilege-escalation exploits for 948 of them, and found 81 previously unknown high-severity Firefox flaws. The model is rated a “critical” cybersecurity risk and is available only to vetted Daybreak Red users.
