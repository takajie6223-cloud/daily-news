Top Story · Nvidia Earnings

Nvidia Beats Big, but Board Shuts the Door on Huang's Return

In its first report since Jensen Huang resigned, Nvidia said third-quarter revenue more than doubled to $115.8 billion and guided fourth-quarter sales to $130 billion — yet its chairman told investors the board has no intention of bringing the founder back.

Source Reuters·The Wall Street Journal

Nvidia reported after Tuesday's close, in its first earnings release since Jensen Huang resigned last week, and beat Wall Street estimates across the board: third-quarter revenue rose 103% from a year earlier to $115.8 billion, and the company forecast fourth-quarter revenue of about $130 billion, plus or minus 3%, above the average analyst estimate of $125.25 billion, with no data-center chip sales to China assumed. Interim chief executive Colette Kress also raised the fiscal 2028 revenue growth outlook to about 75%. Nvidia shares rose nearly 5% in extended trading.

Beyond the numbers, succession remains unresolved. Board chairman Harvey Jones told the earnings call that the board had unanimously accepted Huang's resignation and does 'not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO'; the company has hired an executive-search firm to find a new chief and has created a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee. Meanwhile, The Wall Street Journal reported that the Securities and Exchange Commission is investigating Nvidia, including whether its disclosures misled investors.

NASA says Voyager 1, launched in 1977, will reach one light-day from Earth — about 16 billion miles (26 billion kilometres) — at 5:16 a.m. EST on November 18, the first human-made object to travel so far that its signals take a full 24 hours to reach home.
