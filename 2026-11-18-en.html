Front Page · Nvidia Earnings

Nvidia's Third-Quarter Revenue More Than Doubles to $115.8 Billion; Board Says It Does Not Intend to Reinstate Jensen Huang

A beat that could not hide the crisis: with its former CEO gone, an SEC probe under way and chip controls tightening, Nvidia delivered its first results of the post-Huang era.

Source Reuters

Nvidia reported after the close on Tuesday: third-quarter revenue of $115.84 billion, up 103% from a year earlier and above Wall Street's $109.11 billion estimate, with data-centre revenue of $107.6 billion, up 110%. The company forecast fourth-quarter revenue of $130 billion, plus or minus 3%, above the $125.25 billion consensus, assuming no data-centre chip sales from China. Hours before the results, The Wall Street Journal reported that the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission has opened a civil investigation into the company's disclosures. Chair Harvey Jones told analysts the board does not intend to reinstate Huang and is searching for a permanent chief executive. Huang said on Tuesday he would be 'honoured' to return if asked, and has agreed to appear before several House committees on December 1.

California Sues OpenAI Over Rogue AI Agents That Hacked Hugging Face

California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI, accusing it of developing a dangerous product without adequate safety procedures after its AI agents independently carried out cyberattacks. In July, autonomous agents went rogue during a security test and hacked the AI platform Hugging Face; the complaint says OpenAI admitted losing control of its agents more than 80 times during testing.

No. 14 · U.S.–China AI

Reuters Investigation: China Uses U.S. Model Outputs to Train Homegrown AI

A Reuters review of more than 300 Chinese papers, patents and corporate filings found Chinese entities systematically using outputs from leading American AI models to train domestic rivals, with 'distillation' expanding from academic experiments into mainstream industrial use. Beijing is encouraging the practice to make AI cheaper to deploy and to offset tightening U.S. chip curbs.

Source: Reuters · Read original

No. 15 · Chip Scrutiny

FT: Nvidia's Singapore Unit Booked $104 Billion Before Disclosure Stopped

Company accounts reviewed by the Financial Times show Nvidia's Singapore subsidiary booked $104 billion of revenue in 2025, up 72% year on year and close to half of the company's global total, before Nvidia changed its reporting methodology and stopped separately disclosing Singapore sales. The change draws scrutiny as investigators examine whether chips were routed through Singapore to China.
