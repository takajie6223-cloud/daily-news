Front Page · AI Selloff

U.S. Stocks Plunge in AI Selloff as Nasdaq Drops 5.3% and Turns Negative for the Year

Artificial intelligence stocks tumbled on Friday, with the S&P 500 down 3.7% and the Nasdaq Composite posting its worst week in more than 18 months.

Source Reuters

The selloff in artificial intelligence stocks deepened on Friday, with the Nasdaq Composite plunging 5.3% to close at 22,617.73, wiping out its gains for the year. The S&P 500 fell 3.7% to 6,910.39 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 1.9% to 48,147.33. Nvidia tumbled 14% on the day and 23% for the week, while Anthropic fell about 10% a day after its debut, nearly erasing its first-day gains. Worries over lofty AI valuations, slowing data-centre construction and the prospect of further Federal Reserve rate rises drove investors toward value stocks and government bonds. Even so, many investors said they still believed in the long-term AI story, seeing the rout as a sharp valuation correction rather than a burst bubble.

No. 06 · Chip Curbs

Alibaba Lowers Cloud Growth Outlook, Betting on Homegrown Chips to Offset U.S. Curbs

Alibaba said new U.S. rules targeting Chinese firms' access to high-end Nvidia chips through overseas data centres could slow cloud revenue growth next year to 30% to 45%, down from 53% this quarter. The company kept its goal of $100 billion in external cloud revenue by 2030, saying domestic computing capacity would become the main engine of cloud growth. Tencent likewise said the curbs would slow development of its next-generation AI model, but that its cloud business would be largely unaffected because it mainly runs inference workloads that can operate on domestic chips.

Source: Reuters Breakingviews · Read original

No. 07 · Asia Selloff

Asian Shares Suffer Worst Week Since 2020 as Nikkei Drops 5% in a Day and Taiwan Falls 17% for the Week

Asian shares tumbled on Friday as Anthropic's debut failed to restore confidence in the AI sector. Japan's Nikkei 225 closed 5.06% lower at 56,436.65, its lowest close in seven months; South Korea's Kospi fell 5.2%, and Taiwan shares lost 17.5% for the week, their biggest weekly drop since 1990. The MSCI Asia-Pacific index excluding Japan fell about 8.5% for the week.

Source: Reuters · Read original

No. 08 · Red-Chip Listings

China to Bar Young Red-Chip Firms From Listing Abroad Unless They Re-Domicile at Home

China's securities regulator has told some law firms it will not approve offshore listing plans by red-chip firms established after the 2023 offshore listing rules unless they first re-domicile in China, Reuters reported exclusively. The CSRC has approved Hong Kong listings for just six red-chip companies so far this year, down sharply from 50 last year, a move that could dent Hong Kong's IPO market.
