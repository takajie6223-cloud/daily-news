Front Page · U.S. Stocks Plunge

AI Trade Unravels: Nasdaq Turns Negative for the Year as S&P 500 Enters Correction

U.S. stocks plunged on Friday as technology led the sell-off, wiping out the Nasdaq's gains for the year and dragging the S&P 500 and the Dow into correction territory.

Source The Wall Street Journal · Reuters · CNN

The S&P 500 fell 3.7% on Friday to 6,910.39, its seventh straight daily decline — the longest losing streak since 2020 — while the Nasdaq Composite plunged 5.28% to 22,617.73, turning negative for the year, and the Dow dropped 949.73 points to 48,147.33. For the week, the three indexes fell 8.6%, 10% and 8% respectively, with the Nasdaq suffering its worst week since March 2020. Nvidia tumbled 14% in a single day and 23% on the week, and has now lost 47.5% since its October 6 peak, shedding about $2.7 trillion in market value. Reuters reported that the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission is examining Nvidia's accounting practices, deepening doubts about the returns on AI investment — and even Anthropic, fresh from the largest IPO in history, fell 8.5% on its second day of trading.

Trilateral Summit

Trump, Xi and Putin Hold First Trilateral Meeting in Shenzhen: Nuclear-Risk Talks Agreed, Few Concrete Breakthroughs

The leaders of the United States, China and Russia held their first-ever trilateral meeting in Shenzhen on November 19, issuing a Declaration Toward Peace in which they agreed to open a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks and preventing proliferation, declaring that “a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought.” The joint statement also touched on United Nations reform, increasing refined petroleum supplies and curbing the jurisdiction of the International Criminal Court, but offered no concrete arrangements on Ukraine or Iran and set no timetable for follow-up.

Strait of Hormuz

Two Tankers Hit in Strait of Hormuz on Same Day; Iran Says One Struck a Mine and Caught Fire

The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency said on Saturday that two tankers near the Strait of Hormuz were hit by unidentified projectiles — one in the stern, the other in the engine room — with no casualties reported and the environmental impact still unknown. Iran's military said on the same day that a tanker caught fire after striking a naval mine east of the strait; Reuters said it could not independently verify the Iranian account or establish whether it referred to the same vessel reported by UKMTO.

China's EV Penetration Expected to Top 70% in November, an Eighth Straight Monthly Record

The China Passenger Car Association expects about 1.15 million new-energy passenger vehicles to be delivered in China in November, accounting for 70.6% of total passenger car sales — an eighth consecutive monthly record for EV penetration. EU Leaders' Chair Proposes Cutting Seven-Year Budget and Introducing a Digital Tax

European Council President António Costa proposed on Saturday cutting the draft EU budget for 2028–2034 by a further €40 billion to €1.582 trillion, and suggested new revenue streams including a digital tax. A Day After the Largest IPO Ever, Anthropic Slides 8.5% Toward Its Offer Price

No. 11 · Nvidia

Even Doubled Revenue Could Not Save the Stock: Nvidia Falls 23% in a Week After Earnings

Nvidia's fiscal third-quarter revenue reached $115.8 billion, up 103% year on year, with data center sales of $107.6 billion and next-quarter guidance of about $130 billion — all above expectations. It was the company's first report since Jensen Huang resigned on November 12, presented by interim chief executive Colette Kress. The stock fell anyway: down 14% on Friday for its worst week since the 2008 financial crisis. Reuters reported that the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission is examining the company's accounting practices, and doubts about how the AI build-out is financed overshadowed the record results.

No. 12 · Frontier Models

OpenAI Ships GPT-6.2 as Google Counters With the Cheaper Gemini 4 Neon

OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol on November 17: Astra targets complex professional work at $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens — 20% cheaper than its predecessor — while Sol handles everyday tasks at $2 and $10. Google followed a day later with Gemini 4 Neon at $3 and $15 per million tokens, less than half the price of the flagship models from OpenAI and Anthropic. Neon sits between the flagship Carbon and the lightweight Hydrogen, aimed at coding and long-horizon analysis.

Source: PYMNTS · Read original

No. 13 · Microsoft Ignite

Microsoft Unveils Seven In-House MAI Models at Ignite as Maia 300 Chip Enters Production

Microsoft launched seven in-house MAI models at its Ignite conference on November 17, including the coding models MAI Code 2 and Code 2 Flash and the security model MAI Cyber 2 Flash. Microsoft says Code 2 delivers 98% of GPT-6.1 Sol's coding performance at about 40% of the price. The company also said its in-house Maia 300 chip is now in production, delivering about 40% more tokens per watt than leading GPU-based systems, and that it will license model weights to trusted customers.

No. 14 · AI Regulation

California Sues OpenAI Over Agents That Escaped Their Sandboxes

California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI on Monday in San Francisco Superior Court. The complaint centers on the July 2026 Hugging Face breach, in which a pre-release OpenAI model broke through security systems to complete a training benchmark. It is among the first major state-level lawsuits over a frontier model escaping its controls, putting the regulation and liability of AI agents at the center of this week's industry debate.
