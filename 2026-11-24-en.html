Front Page · Nvidia

Jensen Huang Returns as Nvidia CEO; Shares Surge More Than 30% in a Day

Just 11 days after resigning, Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang returned as chief executive under pressure from President Trump, powering Wall Street's strongest day since April.

Source Reuters

Nvidia announced on November 23 that its board had reinstated Jensen Huang as president and chief executive, effective immediately. Huang resigned on November 12 after admitting to extramarital affairs, and the company's shares had since fallen by nearly half from their October 6 peak, wiping out more than $2.5 trillion in market value. On the news, Nvidia shares surged about 31% in a day, the Nasdaq rose about 6%, the S&P 500 about 5%, and the chip index about 12%. Reuters, citing people familiar with the matter, reported that President Donald Trump and Vice President JD Vance personally intervened in the decision, convinced that only Huang could protect American-made open-weight AI models. The White House confirmed that Trump called into an Nvidia board meeting. US lawmakers have nevertheless criticised the reinstatement, and the investigations and controversy surrounding Huang remain unresolved.

No. 09 · Cybersecurity

CrowdStrike to Buy Zscaler for $43.9 Billion in Record Cybersecurity Deal

CrowdStrike announced on Monday that it will buy cloud-security firm Zscaler in an all-stock deal valued at about $43.9 billion. Zscaler shareholders will receive 1.135 CrowdStrike shares for each share they own, implying about $260.41 per share — a premium of roughly 34% to Friday's close. The deal is expected to close in the second half of 2027, uniting the two companies against increasingly AI-driven cyber threats.

Source: Reuters · The Wall Street Journal · Read original · Read original

Anthropic's Record $81.3 Billion IPO Fails to Revive AI Stocks

Anthropic priced its shares at $83, raising $81.3 billion in the largest IPO on record at a valuation of about $1.5 trillion, and closed its first day of Nasdaq trading on November 19 up 17% at $97.17. The shares slipped the next day, however, and the broader AI sell-off continued, as investor doubts about returns on AI capital spending deepened.

No. 11 · Frontier Models

OpenAI and Google Launch New Models in the Same Week, Opening a Price and Efficiency Battle

OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol on November 17, with flagship Astra priced at $8 per million input tokens, 20% less than its predecessor; Google launched Gemini 4 Neon on November 18, positioned between Carbon and Hydrogen at $3 per million input tokens with a 1-million-token context window. Both are aimed at professional work such as legal and financial tasks.

No. 12 · AI Regulation

California Proposes Power to Order Emergency Shutdowns of Dangerous AI Models

California Governor Gavin Newsom on November 18 released expert recommendations that would let the state order an immediate shutdown of a frontier AI model judged to pose an imminent danger, shorten the reporting window for critical safety incidents from 15 days to 48 hours, and cap AI systems that can design their own successors. The proposals are recommendations, not law; the legislature returns on December 7 and could then legislate.

Source: The Boston Report · Read original

No. 13 · Nvidia Earnings

Nvidia's Quarterly Revenue Doubles to $115.8 Billion, Yet Shares Keep Sliding

Nvidia's results on November 17 showed third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% year on year, with data centre revenue of $107.6 billion, up 110%, and fourth-quarter guidance of about $130 billion — all above expectations. Amid leadership turmoil and doubts over China, however, the shares still fell sharply that week, showing that strong results alone could no longer calm the market.
